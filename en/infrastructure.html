Prosperity · Infrastructure

The arteries of the most advanced nation

From undersea maglev corridors and the world's first public Hyperloop to fusion power, artificial archipelagos and an orbital space station, Waikiki builds the infrastructure of the next century today, financed without borrowing by the National Wealth Fund.

Four pillars of national infrastructure

The Ministry of Infrastructure, led by Secretary Mary Bono Mack, directs the development and maintenance of roads, the railway network, airports, undersea tunnels and port capacity. Strategic projects are approved by the Senate, which decides on the budget of priority investments and monitors their delivery.

Mobility

Maglev and Hyperloop corridors, an undersea link to Florida, and free urban transport made up of metro lines, electric trams and autonomous buses.

Energy

More than ninety percent of supply comes from solar, wind, nuclear and fusion sources, distributed through one of the world's most advanced electrical grids.

Digital

Internet access is a constitutional right confirmed by referendum. Waikiki One brings every public service onto a single, secure citizen platform.

Ports and Logistics

The fully digitalised commercial port of Nova Aurelia, Port Royal's deep-water terminals and smart port logistics in Recife link Waikiki to world trade.

Corridors that unite the provinces

Waikiki's constitution forbids any restriction on the movement of people or goods between provinces. The transport network turns that principle into concrete, steel and magnetic levitation.

  1. Florida-Waikiki Undersea Corridor

    Initiated by Raimondo Chease, the corridor connects the United States with Waikiki's islands. Phase one builds a maglev line on the Santiago-Morón section; phase two completes an undersea highway on the same route.

  2. The world's first public Hyperloop

    Construction began on the outskirts of the capital in Mega Pyramid City, linking the megastructure to central Nova Aurelia in minutes rather than hours.

  3. Free urban transport

    Approved by a large majority in a national referendum, metro lines, electric trams and autonomous buses carry passengers in every major city without fares.

  4. Island links

    An elevated railway serves Palm Island, while bridges and cable cars connect the 60 islands of the World Islands archipelago along its 200 km coastline.

An energy system built for abundance

Under the Patriotic Capitalism doctrine Waikiki harnesses the sun, the wind, the atom and oil alike. Revenues from controlled fossil extraction fund the transition, while fusion plants and vast solar arrays now carry the national load.

Hydrogen fusion since 2017

Waikiki commissioned its first commercial hydrogen fusion power plant in 2017. Today, state programmes operate multiple commercial fusion facilities providing virtually limitless clean energy.

Hydroelectric power

Harnessing the immense river basins of Amazonia and Brazilia, massive hydroelectric stations complement the grid with steady baseload renewable power.

The most advanced grid

An intelligent national grid, backed by 120 hybrid solar and wind Powerpack stations, balances supply across three provinces and two time zones.

Clean Future 2035

Every new public building must carry solar panels, and the national target is a carbon-neutral energy supply by 2035.

Mega Buildings

Announced by Raimondo Chease in 2016, the Mega Buildings programme redefines the nation's skyline across two continents. Each project is designed to be energy-independent and carbon-neutral.

Sky City

At 956 metres and 220 floors, the world's tallest building rises above a vast illuminated musical fountain system.

Mega Pyramid City

Opened in 2019 for the nation's 20th anniversary: a self-sufficient pyramid housing homes, offices and shopping beneath solar-glass facets and vertical gardens.

Princess Tower

At 720 metres and 180 floors, this soaring residential skyscraper in Nova Aurelia features panoramic sky gardens and ultra-fast double-deck elevators.

Commercial Center

The world's largest financial district, anchored by a 650-metre tower dedicated to the national stock exchange.

Hotel Atlantis

An iconic four-tower archipelago resort rising up to 600 metres, combining submerged marine-life suites and high-altitude skybridges.

Royal Clock Tower

The golden-faced jewel of the new government quarter, completed in 2019, with an observation deck over the metropolis and its islands.

Infinity Tower

Completed in 2009 at 480 metres and 93 floors, it stands as the world's only skyscraper with a full 90-degree spiral twist from base to pinnacle.

Miapolis

A vertical city-state of almost 1,000 metres planned for Rio de Janeiro, with its own residential zones, parks and intelligent elevators.

Digital state, orbital reach

Waikiki's most ambitious infrastructure is invisible from the street: a secure digital backbone for every citizen and a permanent presence 200 kilometres above the Earth.

Waikiki One

The integrated citizen platform: identity, tax, healthcare, voting and payments in one secure application.

Digital Education Bridge

Launched in 2020, it delivered tablets and portable modems to students on remote islands and in Amazonia.

National Space Station

A $200 billion orbital complex, served by the Andromeda, Aldrin and Columbus shuttles of the Waikiki Space Agency.

Cyber resilience

Election integrity and digital security are institutional priorities, strengthened by NATO cyber-defence cooperation.
